# Reconstructed PCA detector-space representation

This pipeline postprocesses real mission-generated standard products. It does not claim to rerun raw-event reduction or to produce unfolded photon flux. It uses the linked response for channel-energy assignment and validation; it does not divide count spectra by an arbitrary response curve or numerically invert a response matrix.

For source and background PHA count vectors D and B, exposures tD and tB, area scalings aD and aB, and background scalings bD and bB, the net OGIP rate is

`R = D/(tD aD) − (bD/bB) B/(tB aB)`.

Rates supplied directly in a PHA would omit the corresponding exposure divisor. The current admitted SAEXTRCT subset specifically requires summed integer source counts and an exposure equal to GTI on-time. Division by the explicitly selected PCU count gives the mean recorded rate per selected PCU; the same division applies to errors. Background and source statistical variances are added with **squared** scaling factors. These conventions follow the [XSPEC data-statistics definition](https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/software/xspec/manual/node11.html) and [OGIP spectrum standard](https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/heasarc/ofwg/docs/spectra/ogip_92_007/node7.html).

The transformation is named **PCA_OGIP_RATE43_OVERLAP_V1**. It uses 43 equally wide output intervals from 5 to 25 keV. For every response EBOUNDS interval, its contribution to an output interval is the overlap width divided by its native width. This conserves integrated detector counts under an explicitly stated piecewise-constant density assumption within native channels. It is not an interpolation between energy centers. The approximation is resolution-limited, especially where one native channel overlaps several output bins; the output bins must not be treated as 43 statistically independent measurements.

If W is the overlap matrix and V is the diagonal native statistical covariance, the output is `W R` with covariance `W V Wᵀ`. Both the full covariance and the diagonal error vector are saved. Integrated-rate uncertainties require the full covariance sum, not merely its trace. SEM features use the diagonal formal errors as predictor inputs; this is a classification representation, not a likelihood fit assuming independent rebinned bins. Native boundaries, counts, rates, variances, the transformation matrix, and response checksums remain available for every processed spectrum.

Native grids may change across gain settings and dates. No identical-centers requirement is imposed. The response-specific grid checksum is the calibration identifier; an epoch is not guessed from an unverified date table. The original paper's 43 native count-rate values and this fixed energy-grid representation are related but not identical. The reconstruction configuration was declared before model fitting.

The quality cut retains integrated 5–25 keV net recorded rates greater than 5 count/s/selected PCU. Source, scaled background and net rates are all stored. Negative individual background-subtracted bins are physically possible and are retained. This is a declared adaptation of the reference's 5 count/s rejection, whose text does not specify a fully recoverable identical normalization/band convention. It is not described as exact replication.

Instrument limitations remain material. [NASA's standard-product guide](https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/xte/recipes/stdprod_guide.html) describes generic filtering, detector selection and background models; these are useful archive products, not automatically definitive science reductions. The archived light curves explicitly report DEADAPP=false. No deadtime correction, contemporary response recalibration, burst excision, imaging deblending or new background fit is silently fabricated here. Response EBOUNDS corrects energy assignment but not all detector-efficiency differences. The primary question concerns empirical transfer within this reconstructed detector-space cohort, and cannot by itself prove that learned structure is intrinsic to the compact object.

For a later calibrated reduction, use the instrument team's [PCA preparation/extraction workflow](https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/xte/recipes2/Overview.html) and [pcaextspect2](https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/software/lheasoft/help/pcaextspect2.html) with prepared source and background Standard2 files, deadtime information, filter files and CALDB. HEASoft was not available on the current PATH, and no HEASoft execution is claimed. Such a later reduction is a separately versioned sensitivity analysis, not a retrospective replacement chosen to improve primary scores.
